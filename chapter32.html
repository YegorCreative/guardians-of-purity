Healing the Inner Child

“He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds.”

— Psalm 147:3

Healing begins when we allow God to enter the places we learned to hide—and let Him restore what was wounded long ago.

Parable: The Locked Room

There was a man who lived in a house with many rooms.

One room had been locked since his childhood. Inside were broken toys, old drawings, and memories he did not like to revisit. Whenever the door creaked, he hurried past it, pretending it was not there. Yet the weight of that locked room followed him through the house.

One day, he invited the Master of the house to walk with him. Together, they opened the door. The room was dusty and quiet, but the Master did not turn away. He knelt, lifted what was broken, and restored what had been forgotten.

When they closed the door again, it was no longer locked.

Moral:
Healing begins when we allow God to enter the places we learned to hide—and let Him restore what was wounded long ago.

1. What is the Inner Child?

Introduction: Understanding the Inner Child
The term “inner child” refers to the part of us that carries the experiences, emotions, and memories of our younger selves. Often, unresolved wounds from childhood—whether caused by trauma, neglect, rejection, or unmet emotional needs—can influence our behaviors, thoughts, and relationships in adulthood. Healing the inner child is not just about looking back; it’s about inviting God into those broken places and allowing Him to bring restoration, joy, and freedom.

  • Definition: The inner child represents the vulnerable, innocent, and sometimes wounded version of yourself that formed during childhood.
  • Unhealed Wounds: Experiences like rejection, neglect, or trauma can leave emotional scars that resurface in adulthood, often in the form of unhealthy behaviors or thought patterns.
  • God’s Heart for Your Healing: God desires to heal every part of you, including the hurts you’ve carried from childhood.
“The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because He has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim freedom for the prisoners and recovery of sight for the blind, to set the oppressed free.”
— Luke 4:18

Reflection Questions

  • Are there specific memories or experiences from your childhood that still affect you today?
  • How have these wounds shaped the way you view yourself or others?
  • What areas of your life feel stuck or burdened because of past hurts?

2. Recognizing the Effects of Unhealed Wounds

Unhealed childhood wounds often show up in subtle but impactful ways:

  • Emotional Triggers: Feeling overly sensitive to criticism, rejection, or conflict.
  • Unhealthy Coping Mechanisms: Turning to pornography, masturbation, or other destructive behaviors to numb pain or fill unmet needs.
  • Fear and Insecurity: Struggling with feelings of unworthiness, shame, or abandonment.
  • Difficulty Trusting Others: Building walls in relationships to protect yourself from being hurt again.
“Cast all your anxiety on Him because He cares for you.”
— 1 Peter 5:7

Do This Now

Take a moment to write down one area of your life where you notice the effects of past wounds. Ask God to reveal how these experiences are influencing your thoughts or behaviors.

3. Inviting God into Your Healing Journey

Healing the inner child is not something you do alone. It requires inviting God into your pain and allowing Him to work in your heart.

  • Bring Your Pain to God: Be honest with God about your hurts, fears, and struggles.
“The Lord is close to the brokenhearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit.”
— Psalm 34:18
  • Forgive and Release: Forgive those who hurt you, not because they deserve it, but because it frees you from the burden of bitterness.
  • Embrace God’s Truth: Replace lies you’ve believed about yourself (e.g., “I’m not good enough”) with the truth of God’s Word (e.g., “I am fearfully and wonderfully made”).

Reflection Questions

  • What pain or memories do you need to bring to God in prayer?
  • Are there people you need to forgive to begin the healing process?
  • What truths from Scripture can help you replace lies with God’s perspective?

4. Practical Steps for Healing

  • Journaling: Write letters to your younger self, expressing compassion, understanding, and God’s truth about your value.
  • Therapy and Counseling: Seek out a Christian counselor or therapist who can help guide you through the process of healing.
  • Accountability and Community: Share your journey with a trusted friend, mentor, or spiritual leader who can support you with prayer and encouragement.
  • Grieve and Let Go: Give yourself permission to grieve unmet needs or losses from your childhood, then release them to God.

Do This Now

Write a short prayer or letter to your younger self. Speak to that version of you with kindness, grace, and truth, reminding them of God’s love and faithfulness.

5. Moving Toward Wholeness

Healing the inner child is a process, not an instant transformation. Each step you take toward acknowledging your pain, inviting God into your healing, and embracing His truth moves you closer to wholeness. As you allow God to restore your heart, you’ll find greater freedom, joy, and confidence to walk in His purpose for your life.

“He has made everything beautiful in its time.”
— Ecclesiastes 3:11
